# Persona audit, 2026-09-12

Sixteen financial educators, audited against the households their audiences
actually bring. Four parallel audits, each given a group with their goal, why
they hold it, and who listens, then driven against the running app on the
fixture households that match. Every claim acted on was reproduced first.

## Fixed in this session

- **D-223** A month that does not close outranks every optimisation. A family
  short $4,997 a month was told to point their remaining cash at a credit
  card. Found for Caleb Hammer and Dave Ramsey.
- **D-224** One definition of what leaves each month. When It Won't All Get
  Paid told a household $415 short that it had $1,800 left; the ladder
  charged homeowners their mortgage twice. Found from four directions.
- **D-225** Drawing It Down opened only once retirement was irreversible, and
  the FOO ladder judged high-interest debt at 6% while every other room read
  7.5%. Found for Two Sides of FI and The Money Guys.
- **D-226** Variable Income printed a high month lower than its average.
  Found for Gary Vaynerchuk's audience.

## Recurring themes, still open

1. **The parts exist; the wiring does not.** Social Security never reaches
   the FI number. Withdrawal ordering is built and used only for emergencies.
   The Target Monthly Income never meets the FI number. The fixed-cost floor
   never sets an owner's salary. Known problem 4 in ARCHITECTURE.md.
2. **Fields that gate whole features and nothing writes.** `meta.remoteOk`
   kills geoarbitrage; missing contributions stop the ladder at step 4.
3. **A business cannot be represented.** No asset category, no ownership
   share, no valuation, no owner draw, no business debt. Four of the sixteen
   speak mostly to owners.
4. **One person stands in for the household.** Nineteen engines read the
   first adult's age; two retirement dates cannot be entered.
5. **Anything needing a past is dead on arrival.** No way to enter history,
   so five features are permanently empty.
6. **Onboarding ends in a completion meter, not an answer.**

## What to do next

1. Feed Social Security into the FI number; show 62/67/70 side by side.
2. Ask "is my work remote" and "what do you contribute" — two inputs, two
   complete features.
3. Put the FI number beside the Target Monthly Income.
4. Let a business be an asset.
5. Give the second adult their own age and retirement date.
6. Let someone type last year's numbers in.
7. Price a raise over a career, and a recurring expense at 25x.
8. End onboarding with an answer.

## What they would praise

No room invents a number it does not have. Blank is never quietly zero.
Benefits, premiums, marginal rates and the sequence-risk gauge are refused
with a reason rather than guessed. Nothing suggests borrowing to invest or
to start a business. Every one of these sixteen serves people at or below
zero at some point, and the app does not break there.
