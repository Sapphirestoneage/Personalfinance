Get Help

Every room has a line saying what it does not do. This is where those lines point: the questions that need a person, and what kind.

What these rooms do not do
  • Tax returns and filings

    Every tax figure here is an estimate from a flat effective-rate table. Nothing here files anything, handles state quirks, or knows your deductions.

  • Investment picks

    The rooms model returns as one assumed rate. Which fund, which account, which day — not here.

  • Insurance quotes and claims

    The Coverage Checkup reads what you hold against what it would have to carry; it cannot price a policy or judge a claim.

  • Legal documents

    Estate Basics asks whether a will, beneficiaries and a power of attorney exist. Writing them is a lawyer's work, or a service that is one.

  • Debt in crisis

    The payoff room orders what you owe. Collections, bankruptcy, a hardship plan — those need someone who does it every day.

  • Bank connections

    Nothing here links to an account. Every number is typed, pasted, or guessed and marked as one.

Who answers them
  • A fee-only fiduciary planner

    Paid by you, not by what they sell, and bound to act in your interest. Ask both questions before anything else. One-off, hourly and flat-fee engagements exist.

  • An enrolled agent or a CPA

    For the return, a notice, self-employment estimates, or a year with a sale, a move, or an inheritance in it.

  • A non-profit credit counsellor

    For debt that has stopped being a spreadsheet problem. Look for non-profit status and a fee schedule up front.

  • An estate lawyer

    For the will, the trust, the power of attorney, and who gets what — especially with children, property or a business.

  • Your state's unemployment office

    For the benefit itself: eligibility, the weekly amount, the weeks. The Between Jobs room reads a cap; the office decides.
