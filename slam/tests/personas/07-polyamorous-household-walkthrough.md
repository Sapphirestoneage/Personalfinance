# Walkthrough: Tess, Omar and Lin, polyamorous household

Method and shared findings: `UI-NOTES.md`. Hand math: `07-polyamorous-household.expected.md`.

## What the pages showed

- Ledger: loaded, 104 fields, two people under "You and where you file", Lin present only as a name in the covered-by list. The four shared bills read "$3,900 a month, shared by 3: you pay $2,600 a month", which is exactly right for a two-thirds share typed as a percent.
- Dashboard: loaded (Omar's contract income is rough). Net worth $144,600. Runway 2.8 months. Surplus **-$176** (after a $1,200 "minimum" on the Visa paid in full and a $928 set-aside). Savings rate 6.8%. FI 7.7% of $1,812,198 (today's spending). 2026 balance due $6,499 (New York City tax not included).
- By hand: the surplus is $686 (business expenses taken out, no Visa double count, a $666 set-aside after the SEP and health insurance deductions); the balance due is $4,661.

## No field for

- A third adult. Lin has income, accounts, debts and a third of four bills. The only place for her is "covered by: Lin" on an expense, which would make the whole bill hers. The two-thirds share works because "your share" can be a percent, but the whole-house view (three incomes, the bills once) does not exist, and a per-return view for Lin would be a second file with the same bills typed again.
- A sharing formula. Two-thirds is typed as 66.67% on each of four bills; "by income" or "two of three adults" is not a thing the app knows, so a change means four edits.
- Omar's deductions: the SEP IRA contribution ($4,800 a year) and self-employed health insurance ($5,760) are not deducted; his business expenses do not reduce the month's money (gross $4,200 counts in take-home).
- Quarterly estimates for Omar: a date, no amount.

## Forced into the wrong category

- Omar's SEP IRA is in the IRA family, so its contribution counts as after-tax saving; it is pre-tax.
- Lin as a "payer" name sits in a list with Parents and Employer.

## Confusing labels or steps

- "How many people share it, you included?" with "I" meaning the couple. The question is written for one person; for a married couple sharing with a third adult, "you" means two people and the share is two-thirds, which takes a moment to see.

## Results that look wrong or misleading

- The surplus sign is wrong (negative shown, positive by hand).
- Runway 2.8 months counts the couple's cash against bills that include Lin's third only when she stops paying; fine, but a "what if Lin moves out" needs four edits.

## Assumptions that are not true for them

- A household is one or two adults who file together. Three adults, two returns and one home does not fit.
- "You" is one person.

## Rating

**Works with workarounds** for the couple's own view (shares as percents, the file for the second person); **doesn't work** for the household as they live it.
