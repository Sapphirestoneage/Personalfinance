# Regular FIRE: Wes Delgado (persona 17, 38, made up)

Reuses the golden household `../golden/households/w2-wes.json` (hand math in
`../golden/households/w2-wes.expected.md`, engine comparison in
`../golden/w2-wes.golden.test.js`). Walkthrough: `17-regular-fi-walkthrough.md`.

## Who he is

Wes is 38, a software developer in Buffalo on $83,200, single, renting. He is
the textbook case: a 401(k) with a 50%-of-6% match, a Roth IRA, a brokerage
account, a rewards card paid in full every month, no other debt, a 28% savings
rate, and an FI number he set himself at $1,100,000 (a bit above the $969,600
his spending implies, to leave room). He expects to get there in his late
fifties and would take earlier.

## Every number he would enter

The golden file holds them: a paycheck (W-2) every two weeks with
withholding; a 401(k) with a monthly contribution and the match as text; a
Roth IRA with a basis; a brokerage account; rent, utilities, internet,
renters insurance, groceries, eating out, metro pass, gym, phone, streaming,
everything else; a checking account and a high-yield savings account; one
credit card with the pay setting "the full statement balance"; an FI goal as
a direct number.

## What he would want the app to tell him

- Years to FI and the date, with the match counted (it is a quarter of his retirement saving and the app leaves it out).
- His real surplus and runway: the card he pays in full is not a $900 debt payment on top of the spending it already is.
- The savings rate as he thinks of it: everything saved, including the match, over gross.
- Whether the Roth IRA still makes sense against a brokerage account at his bracket, and what the 22% marginal rate means for pre-tax versus Roth 401(k).
- A single clear answer to "if I keep doing exactly this, when".
