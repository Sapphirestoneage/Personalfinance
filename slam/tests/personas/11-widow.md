# Widow: Ruth Castellano (persona 11, 63, made up)

File: `households/11-widow.json`. Walkthrough: `11-widow-walkthrough.md`.

## Who she is

Ruth is 63 and lives in the house in Suffolk County she and Paul paid off
years ago. Paul died on June 15, 2026, at 65, still working. Her income was a
part-time library job ($26,400 a year); his was $102,000. Since August she
gets a survivor Social Security benefit of $2,300 a month and half of his
pension, $1,100. His $350,000 group life insurance paid out in August; it sits
in a high-yield savings account ($230,000, after the funeral and some bills)
and a brokerage account ($150,000). His 401(k), $410,000, was rolled into an
IRA in her name. She stayed on his employer's plan through COBRA ($780 a
month) and will need the marketplace from January 2028 until Medicare at 65.

For 2026 she files jointly, as the law allows; from 2027 she files single,
with a smaller standard deduction and the same house. Her questions are
sudden and practical: can she stop the library job, is the life insurance
money taxable (no), how much of the Social Security is, when to switch from
the survivor benefit to her own, and what to do with the IRA before required
distributions begin.

## What she would want the app to tell her

- Can she stop working: $3,400 a month of benefits and pension against $3,860 of spending, with $1.4 million behind it. The app's FI number says 56%; the real answer is about drawing $600 a month from a large portfolio until COBRA ends, then nothing.
- How much of the Social Security is taxable (up to 85% at her income) and what 2027 looks like as a single filer.
- When to switch from the survivor benefit to her own retirement benefit (at 67 or 70 if hers would be larger), which the app has no field for.
- Required minimum distributions on the inherited money from 73, and whether Roth conversions in the low-income years before then make sense.
- Health insurance from January 2028 to April 2028 (Medicare at 65) and what it costs.
