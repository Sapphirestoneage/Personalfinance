File: `households/21-sparks.json`. Walkthrough: `21-sparks-walkthrough.md`.

## Who they are

Kai is 26, a software engineer in Brooklyn whose pay jumped from $72,000 to
$132,000 last year. They live with a roommate, travel, climb, go to shows,
and still save about $2,000 a month without feeling it. Three big moves are
on the table at once: a job offer in Austin at $144,000 with no state tax
(40% they take it), a house hack (a two-family in Queens, FHA, about $60,000
needed by the end of 2027), and a freelance app business that just started
bringing in $800 a month. They do not have an FI number and do not want to
be told to pick one before they know what their life looks like.

This is the launch window: the years when a raise, a move or a business can
change the slope more than any budget line. They want the app to compare the
moves, not to trim the going-out line.

## What they would want the app to tell them

- The three moves side by side: Austin (more pay, no state tax, a new rent), the house hack (a mortgage, rental income, a new city borough), the business (income that could replace the salary). The app has no scenario beyond the Best, Likely and Worst assumption sets.
- A down payment date at the current pace, which the app does not compute for a savings goal with a date.
- An FI picture that does not need a number yet: the implied number from today's spending is shown, but it is $1,497,600 for a life they have not chosen.
- The real surplus. The app says minus $1,680 because the Visa they pay in full is counted as a $1,900 payment; without it they are $220 ahead after $2,067 of saving.
- Quarterly tax on the new 1099 income, and what the Austin move does to withholding mid-year.
