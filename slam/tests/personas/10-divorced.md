# Divorced: Marcus Bell (persona 10, 44, made up)

File: `households/10-divorced.json`. Walkthrough: `10-divorced-walkthrough.md`.

## Who he is

Marcus is 44, an analyst at a state agency in Albany ($84,000), divorced in
2022. His daughter Zoe (11) and son Eli (8) are with him 60% of the time, so
he files as head of household. He pays $1,200 a month in alimony until June
2029 (a post-2018 divorce, so it is not deductible) and receives $900 a month
in child support (not taxable). The decree splits the kids' medical costs
and lets the parents alternate claiming them. He is still paying the divorce
lawyer, $300 a month with no interest.

He has a 457(b) at work, a Roth IRA, a car loan, and a Visa at 29% of its
limit. His questions are about the shape of the next three years: how the
alimony ending changes things, whether the child support belongs in income
(it does not for tax; it does for cash), and how to plan for two sets of costs
in two homes.

## What he would want the app to tell him

- A tax estimate that knows child support is not income and alimony is not deductible, and that includes the child tax credit ($4,400 in a year he claims both) and the dependent care credit.
- What June 2029 does: $1,200 a month back, and an FI number that should not include a cost that ends.
- Head of household: whether he qualifies in a year the ex claims the kids (he does, by custody, not by the exemption).
- A cash view of the two homes: what the kids cost him, what the support covers.
- Whether to pay the lawyer faster (no interest) or the Visa (23.49%). The app's order puts the Visa first, which is right, but the lawyer's plan should not look like a debt that grows.
