# Walkthrough, Yeshivish family: the Weinbergs (persona 09)

Method and shared findings: `UI-NOTES.md`.

## What the pages showed

- Ledger: loaded; eight people listed; the kollel stipend, parents' help and SNAP all under "Other income" next to each other.
- Dashboard: **crashed** (New Jersey).
- Engines directly: gross $7,100 counting the stipend, the gift and the benefits as income; take-home $6,630; expenses $6,860; surplus **-$916**; a $501 set-aside built from 12% of the stipend, the gift and the benefits (the marginal method, because the balance due cannot be figured for New Jersey); no tax number; runway 0.6 months; FI 0.2% of $2,058,000; the projection's first shortfall April 2027.
- By hand: with six children and $85,000 of income their 2026 federal return is a refund of roughly $10,000 or more (child tax credit for six, the earned income credit, the additional child tax credit), not a balance due with a set-aside. The app cannot see any of it.

## No field for

- Benefits. SNAP and WIC are not income and not taxable; entered as "other income" they are taxed and set aside for.
- A gift from family. Taxed as other income.
- A stipend whose taxability is the question. Taxed as other income, no note.
- Six dependents. Birth dates only.
- A gemach: entered as a "family IOU" with interest "not for me", which works, but the name is a stretch and the pick list says "Money owed to family or friends".
- Kollel as a situation: employed, self employed, between jobs, student, retired, mixed. "Student" is closest and wrong for tax.
- A benefits cliff: what a raise does to FamilyCare and SNAP.

## Forced into the wrong category

- Tuition, maaser, shul, yom tov: "Everything else".
- Benefits and gifts: "Other income".

## Results that look wrong or misleading

- A set-aside of $501 a month for tax they do not owe, taken from a household already $900 short on the app's reading.
- "Cash runs out April 2027" for a family whose year balances on a refund the app cannot compute.

## Assumptions that are not true for them

- Income is earned and taxable.
- The household is in New York.
- The number of children does not matter.
- Spending beyond food, housing and transportation is a want.

## Rating

**Doesn't work.**
