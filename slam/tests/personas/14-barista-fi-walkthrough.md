# Walkthrough, Barista FIRE: Theo Marsh (persona 14)

Method and shared findings: `UI-NOTES.md`.

## What the pages showed

- Ledger: loaded, 75 fields.
- Dashboard: loaded. Net worth $745,000. Runway 14.4 months (green). Surplus **-$1,838**. Savings rate **-111.4% of gross**. FI 71.7% of $962,500 ("FI in about 5.8 years" on the Likely set; 9.6 years at his own 3.5% rate and 5% return). 2026 balance due $464 with a $66 set-aside.
- The projection: "Cash runs out December 2028", with $690,000 invested untouched.

## No field for

- A withdrawal. The $1,750 a month he sells from the brokerage account has no kind: not income, not a contribution, not a transfer. The app sees a part-time wage against full spending and reports a household in collapse.
- A Barista FI number: the portfolio only has to cover (spending minus net pay). By first principles that is ($3,205 - $1,434) x 12 / 3.5% = $607,000, which he has. The app's number covers everything, $962,500 at 4% (the Likely set) or $1,100,000 at his 3.5%.
- Capital gains on the draw, the 0% long-term band at his income, and a tax-aware order of accounts.
- A situation for this: "mixed" is the closest word; "retired" would be wrong for the tax engine too.

## Forced into the wrong category

- The two-year cash cushion for the gap is a high-yield savings account that counts as an emergency fund (runway 14.4 months); it is the draw's buffer.

## Results that look wrong or misleading

- Surplus -$1,838 and savings rate -111% for a plan that is working as designed.
- "Cash runs out December 2028": the projection never sells an investment, so cash goes negative while $690,000 compounds.
- "FI in 5.8 years" for someone who does not need full FI.

## Assumptions that are not true for him

- Everyone is accumulating. The engines have no decumulation: no draw, no selling, no required distributions, no Roth conversion ladder.
- Income from work must cover spending.

## Rating

**Doesn't work.** The Ledger holds his numbers; every headline on the Dashboard misreads them.
