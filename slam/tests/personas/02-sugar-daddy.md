# Sugar daddy: Raymond Feld (persona 02, 58, made up)

File: `households/02-sugar-daddy.json`. Walkthrough: `02-sugar-daddy-walkthrough.md`.

## Who he is

Raymond is 58, divorced since 2021, and a vice president at an asset
manager in Manhattan: $336,000 in salary and about $150,000 a year of RSUs
that vest each quarter. He owns his co-op outright, pays $3,500 a month in
alimony until mid-2029 (a 2021 divorce, so it is not deductible), and has a
grown daughter. For two years he has been in an arrangement with a woman in
her late twenties: a $4,000 monthly allowance, plus dinners, travel and gifts
that run another $1,500 a month. He is clear-eyed about it; he budgets it as a
line like any other and does not want it hidden or moralized.

He is five to seven years from stopping work. His questions are the ones a
high earner with a fixed, large discretionary line has: is this line
sustainable next to the FI number, what does it do to the date, how the gift
tax reporting works, and how to keep this part of his life separate from the
money his daughter will one day see.

## What he would want the app to tell him

- Whether $5,500 a month of arrangement spending fits inside a $5,000,000 FI plan, and what the FI date is with it and without it.
- The gift tax side: $48,000 a year to one person is over the $19,000 annual exclusion, so a Form 709 is due each year even though no tax is owed until the lifetime exemption is used. Nowhere in the app asks who receives money.
- His real tax picture: the 0.9% additional Medicare tax, the 3.8% net investment income tax on $102,000 of dividends and gains, the 20% long-term rate band, and New York City tax. Two of those four are not modeled.
- That alimony is not deductible (post-2018 divorce) and ends in 2029, which lifts his surplus by $3,500 then.
- How to keep this line out of any shared or family view later (the app has a single household and a single viewer).
