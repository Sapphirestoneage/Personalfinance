## Who he is

Carlos is 23, works hourly at a retail store in the Bronx ($1,480 every two
weeks before tax) and drives for a delivery app on weekends for about $350 a
month in cash. He shares a two-bedroom with a friend and is on his mother's
health plan until 26. Two years of small emergencies and one bad stretch put
him where he is: a Visa at $4,800 of a $5,000 limit (some of it cash
advances), a store card at $1,350 of $1,500, a $600 payday loan due in two
weeks with a $75 fee, $420 of buy now pay later on sneakers and a phone, and
$1,200 he owes his mother. He has $365 in the bank.

He is not careless; he is underpaid and one step behind. He wants to know,
in order: what gets paid first, when the payday loan ends, whether he can
stop the cards from growing, and how long until he is at zero.

## Every number he would enter

The numbers are in the golden file. In the app's words: a paycheck (W-2) every
two weeks; freelance or contract (1099) for the delivery app, rough; rent
and utilities shared with one other person, an even split; groceries, eating
out and delivery, subway, phone, streaming and games, everything else; a
checking account and a savings account; two credit cards (one with no minimum
entered); a payday or cash advance loan; buy now, pay later; money owed to
family; health coverage "parent plan".

## What he would want the app to tell him

- Which debt first. The avalanche order (payday 390%, store 31.99%, Visa 28.99%) is right; he would also want the payday loan called out as "pay this in full on the 6th or it rolls".
- A month-by-month picture of the first six months: the November shortfall, when the payday and the buy now pay later end, when the surplus turns positive.
- That his cards are at 96% and 90%, what that does to his score, and the number that gets each under 30%.
- A plan that uses his mother: she would wait on the $50 a month; the app treats the family IOU like any loan.
- The tax on the delivery income (it is 1099 and nothing is withheld), as a set-aside he can see.
