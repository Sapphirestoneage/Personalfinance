# Persona 4: Jordan Avery, 28, trans (made up)

File: `households/04-trans-person.json`. Walkthrough: `04-trans-person-walkthrough.md`.

## Who they are

Jordan is 28, a product designer at a mid-size company in Brooklyn making
$64,800. They started hormone therapy two years ago; the employer plan
covers it with copays. Electrolysis is not covered and runs $250 a month. They
have a surgery scheduled for March 2027: the plan covers most of it, so the
cost to them is the $4,500 out-of-pocket maximum plus about $3,000 for parts
the plan excludes, and they will be on unpaid leave for about three weeks
after. They are also changing their legal name this winter: court filing fee,
certified copies, a new passport, a new license, about $450 all told.

Jordan has a surgery fund in a high-yield savings account, a 401(k) at the
match, a Roth IRA, a Visa balance and federal student loans on the standard
plan. Their questions are practical and dated: is the surgery fund on track
for March, how to handle three unpaid weeks, whether an FSA or HSA would help
next year, and what the Visa is costing them while the surgery fund earns 3.9%.

## What they would want the app to tell them

- Whether $7,000 plus what they can add by March reaches $12,000, and what to cut if not.
- What three unpaid weeks do to April 2027, and whether the fund should cover that too.
- A medical total: therapy, HRT, electrolysis and the surgery in one place, since that is what an FSA election or an HSA would be sized to.
- Whether the Visa at 24.49% should come before the surgery fund earning 3.9%, given the date.
- That the name change and the surgery are one-time costs, not new monthly lines, and that the projection shows the dip and the recovery.
