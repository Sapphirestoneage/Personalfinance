# Hallway test: Money Rooms v3 in one hour

A do-it-yourself usability test in the spirit of Krug's "Don't Make Me Think",
chapter 9. Three people, one morning, no lab. The point is not to prove the
app works. The point is to watch three strangers get stuck and write down
where.

## Who to recruit

1. Someone in their twenties who avoids money. Does not check their balance,
   pays the minimum, changes the subject when rent comes up.
2. Someone in their twenties who likes numbers. Has a spreadsheet, knows
   their rate, will argue with a chart.
3. One coach. Anyone who has run a one-on-one call about money, not Eli.

Why these three: the first finds what scares people off, the second finds
what is wrong or unclear in the math, the coach finds what breaks the rhythm
of a live call. You do not need target users to find the big problems. A
cousin or the person at the next desk hits the same walls a real client
hits. Three people find most of what matters; you fix those first.

## Setup (ten minutes)

- A laptop with the app open at `#/home`. Click "Load demo client" so Maya,
  the Jersey City client, is loaded. Turn on Presenting in the header. Check
  that no other client's name shows anywhere.
- Screen recording on (QuickTime, Loom, or the phone propped on a cup), or a
  notepad and a pen if you would rather not record.
- A second chair beside the laptop, not across the table. You sit and watch.
  You do not touch the trackpad.

Facilitator script, said once at the start, in these words or close:

"Thanks for doing this. I am testing the app, not you. There are no wrong
answers and you cannot break anything. I will give you a few small tasks.
Please think out loud as you go: what you are looking at, what you expect,
what confuses you. I cannot help you during the tasks, so if you get stuck,
say so and do your best. When you are done, say 'done'. Any questions before
we start?"

## The tasks

Read each task word for word and start the timer when you finish reading. Do
not reset the app between tasks. If the person goes somewhere strange, let them.

### Client tasks (recruits 1 and 2)

Begin each client session on `#/onepager`.

| # | Task, word for word | Time box | Should end on | Success looks like |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | "Here is your one page. What is the one thing you should do this week?" | 2 min | `#/onepager` | They point at or read "The one thing" and say the action in their own words, without scrolling past it. |
| C2 | "How much can you safely spend today?" | 2 min | `#/calendar` | They reach the calendar and read the "Safe to spend today" number. One click from the one page or the home shelf counts as success. |
| C3 | "When will your Nashville fund be ready?" | 3 min | `#/goals` | They reach the goal timeline, find the Nashville row and say a month and year. Bonus if they notice "Your next win is ..." and say whether Nashville is the next win or a later one. |
| C4 | "Find what moves your FI date most." | 3 min | `#/levers` | They open "What matters most" and name the top row. They do not need to know what FI stands for; if they ask, write it down and say "do your best". |

### Coach task (recruit 3)

Begin the coach session on `#/home`, Presenting still on.

| # | Task, word for word | Time box | Should end on | Success looks like |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| K1 | "Run the first five minutes of session 1." | 8 min | `#/call` | They find "Start the call", open session 1 and move through the first stop or two speaking to an imagined Maya. They do not have to finish. Success is that they never stop to ask you how the runner works. |

Afterwards ask the coach: "Was there a moment you would have reached for a
pen and paper instead?" Write the answer down word for word.

## What to watch for

- Hesitation. The cursor hovers, nothing is clicked, three seconds pass.
- Wrong clicks. They open the Scoreboard when they wanted the calendar.
- Reading labels aloud in a puzzled voice. "Safe to spend ... today?"
- Scrolling past the answer. The number was on the screen and they kept going.
- Asking what a word means. FI, cushion, surplus, lever, snapshot, Barista.
- Saying "I guess" or "I think this is it" and stopping. That is a miss
  wearing a success costume. Write it down as a miss.

Each time one happens, write the screen, the word and the time on the clock.
Sort it later.

## What not to say

- No hints. Not even "it is near the top".
- No explaining. If they ask what a word means, say "what do you think it
  means?" and write down their answer. That answer is the finding.
- No defending the design. Do not say "most people find that". Do not say
  "that is coming in the next version". Nod, write, say "thank you, keep going".
- No leading. "Did you find that confusing?" plants the answer. Ask "what was
  going through your mind there?" instead.

## Debrief template

Fill one of these per person right after their session, before the next one
arrives. Use their words, not yours, for the problem.

| Person | Problem, in their words | Where (screen) | Severity |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 avoids money | | | stopped / slowed / annoyed |
| 2 likes numbers | | | stopped / slowed / annoyed |
| 3 coach | | | stopped / slowed / annoyed |

Three rows per person; copy the row twice more.

Severity means: stopped them (could not finish the task), slowed them (got
there, took the long way), annoyed them (got there, grumbled).

Then, with all nine rows in front of you, pick the top three overall. A
problem two people hit outranks a problem one person hit. "Stopped" outranks
"slowed". Write them here:

| Rank | Problem | Smallest fix that would make it go away | Owner | By |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | | | | |
| 2 | | | | |
| 3 | | | | |

Fix only the top three overall. Resist the rest. Test again next month with
three new people and the same tasks. If a problem from this round is gone, it
is gone; if it is still there, it moves up the list.

## The one-hour schedule

Setup happens in the ten minutes before the first person arrives.

| Time | What |
|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:15 | Session 1: the person who avoids money. Tasks C1 to C4. |
| 0:15 to 0:30 | Session 2: the person who likes numbers. Tasks C1 to C4. |
| 0:30 to 0:45 | Session 3: the coach. Task K1 and the pen-and-paper question. |
| 0:45 to 1:00 | Debrief. Fill the tables above. Pick the top three. Stop. |

Three sessions of fifteen minutes, then fifteen minutes of debrief. Write the
top three on a card and tape it above the laptop until they are fixed.
