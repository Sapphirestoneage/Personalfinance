How it goes

Three ways in, one road

  1. Five questions, one answer.

    Age, what comes in, what goes out, what you have saved, what you owe. That is enough for a first FI date, shown as a band rather than a single false-precise year. The first round.

  2. Every room fills in.

    Each number has one owner. Change your spending in Expenses and the Number, the Scorecard and the Back Half all move. Nothing is asked twice.

  3. The next thing money should do.

    The ladder says which step you are on and what it takes to climb. The dashboard says the date it all points to. The dashboard.

Not sure yet? Every room has a "Try with example numbers" button. Robin Sparks is a made-up household on a $72,000 salary; use them to see the shape of a plan before you type your own.

The rules it keeps

Built for people who count

  • Nothing leaves your browser. No account, no server, no analytics, no network call after the page loads. Your data lives in this browser and leaves only as a file you make.
  • Empty is not zero. A blank box means "not entered yet". A number you have not given is never quietly treated as nothing.
  • One formula, one function. The FIRE number on this site, in the room and on the coach's console is the same calculation, run from the same file.
  • Every number says how old it is. A reading from a figure you typed six months ago says so.
  • Example numbers only, on purpose. This is a public project. No real household's figures are anywhere in it.
